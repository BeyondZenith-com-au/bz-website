Claude for business: what to check before your prototype goes live

A Claude prototype can work in a week. That is the easy part. The hard part is the day after, when staff or customers start to depend on it.

By Anastas Manojlovski · 20 November 2026

Here is what we check before a Claude project goes live. Almost none of it is about the prompt.

1. Use the right plan or platform

Is there a business account for Claude? Yes. There are two ways to use Claude at work, and they suit different jobs.

  • For your staff to use Claude directly, Anthropic offers Team (for teams of 2 to 150 people) and Enterprise plans. On both, Anthropic's pricing page says there is "no model training on your content by default".
  • For Claude inside your own product or systems, you call Claude through an API: the Claude API from Anthropic, or a cloud platform such as Amazon Bedrock.

A prototype often starts on a personal account. Move it to a business plan or your own cloud account before real data goes in.

2. Why we recommend Amazon Bedrock

We are an AWS-first company. For most businesses that build Claude into their own systems, we recommend Amazon Bedrock. Here is why.

  • It runs in your own AWS account. Your AI sits next to your data and your systems, under the same account structure, billing and controls that your team already manages.
  • You use the security controls that you already know. Access is controlled with AWS IAM, so you decide exactly who and what can call each model. AWS CloudTrail records the calls, so you have an audit trail.
  • Your prompts stay away from the model provider. AWS says Bedrock "does not share your content with model providers". Anthropic does not see your prompts or outputs.
  • You can keep requests in Australia with the Australian routing option (see the next section).
  • AWS has well-established practices for AI. Its guidance on security, operations and cost gives your team a proven way to run AI in production, not a new set of rules to invent.
  • You are not locked to one model. Bedrock offers models from several providers, so you can compare and change models without a new contract.

Other cloud providers also offer Claude and other strong models, for example Google Cloud and Microsoft Azure. If your business is already committed to one of them, that can be the right choice. We are happy to talk through the options with you.

3. Know where your data goes

For many Australian businesses, this is the first question from the board or the risk team.

On Amazon Bedrock, you choose how requests are routed. AWS documents three options:

  • The Australian option (au.) "routes only among Australian Regions".
  • The Asia Pacific option (apac.) can route to "any supported Asia Pacific Region". That includes countries other than Australia.
  • The global option (global.) can route to "any supported AWS commercial Region worldwide". AWS says it costs about 10% less.

If your data must stay in Australia, use the Australian option. Then confirm where it routes. AWS itself warns: "Don't rely on the geographic prefix in an inference profile ID alone to determine where requests can be routed."

Also check the data retention settings. Some of the newest Claude models on Bedrock need a mode where AWS can keep prompts and outputs "for up to 30 days" for human review. Check this for the model you choose.

4. Measure accuracy on your own data

A demo shows that Claude can get the answer right. Production needs to know how often it does.

Collect real examples from your business, with the answers a person would accept. Run them before go-live, and again every time the prompt, the data or the model changes. Write down the result. "It looked good in testing" is not a number.

5. Security: more than a password

AI adds new ways for things to go wrong. Before go-live, check that:

  • The AI can reach only what the job needs. If it can read your whole SharePoint or send email, ask why.
  • It treats what it reads as untrusted. A customer email or a document can contain hidden instructions. This is called prompt injection, and it needs controls, not just a careful prompt.
  • Guardrails filter what goes in and what comes out, for example personal information or topics that it must not discuss.
  • Every action is logged, so you can see what happened when someone asks.
  • Someone has tried to break it before your customers do.

How we help: we review the design, test it against common AI attacks, and give you a prioritised list of fixes.

6. Compliance: map it before the auditor asks

AI does not replace the rules that you already follow. It adds new places where you must show that you follow them. Map the project against:

  • the Privacy Act, for any personal information that the AI reads, stores or produces
  • your customer contracts and terms, which may limit how you use customer data
  • for APRA-regulated entities, CPS 234 Information Security and CPS 230 Operational Risk Management, including the risks from AI service providers
  • your own policies on records, data retention and customer communication.

How we help: we map your AI project to your obligations and identify the gaps. Then you can show your board, and your auditor, how the risks are managed. If you need the bigger picture, read AI governance for Australian mid-sized businesses.

7. Running cost

Cost depends on the model, the length of each request and how many requests you get. A larger model is not always better value. Test with realistic volumes and compare a smaller model before you choose. Then set budgets and alerts, so a busy month does not surprise you.

8. Support after go-live

AI models change fast. Anthropic released three new Claude models between 22 September and 7 October 2026. Plan for:

  • an owner who watches accuracy, approves changes and answers when it gets something wrong
  • monitoring of accuracy, errors, response time and cost
  • a process for model upgrades: test the new model on your examples before you switch
  • a plan for incidents: how you turn the feature off, tell users and fix it.
