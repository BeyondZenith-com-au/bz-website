AI for claims and renewals in a brokerage: what to build first, and how to keep client data safe

Start with the work that reads and sorts. Let AI draft, let a person approve, and keep client data in Australia.

By Anastas Manojlovski · 26 November 2026

Contents

  1. The question behind the question
  2. Three places to start
  3. Five rules to keep client data safe
  4. What moves the price
  5. How to get to a fixed answer
  6. Questions brokers ask

Short answer. Start with the work that reads and sorts: claims emails, renewal preparation and certificates of currency. Let AI draft and sort, and let a person approve. A first working flow can be tested within two weeks on test data. Keep client data in your own cloud account in Australia, and never put it into a public chatbot.

The question behind the question

Brokers ask two questions together. “Can AI take over claims triage, renewals and certificates?” And straight after: “Is our client data safe if we do?” Both are right. The value is in the volume of emails and documents. The risk is the same client data.

So the question is not “should we use AI?” It is “which job do we give it first, and what must stay with a person?”

Three places to start

1. Claims emails and documents

AI reads each new claim email and its attachments. It finds the client and the policy, takes out the key facts (date, type of loss, amount), files the email to the right client, and drafts an acknowledgement. A person checks and sends.

  • Cost: a clickable prototype is $5k–10k. A first version is usually $20k–45k. See what a broker system costs.
  • Good for: brokerages and broker networks with many claim emails each day, often into shared inboxes.
  • Watch for: the wrong client match. An email filed to the wrong client is a privacy breach. AI must never make the claim decision. When you act for your client, you are their advocate and the insurer decides. When you handle claims under an insurer’s authority, the decision is a regulated claims handling service, and a person must make it.

2. Renewal preparation

Before each renewal, AI collects the policy, past claims, notes and emails, and drafts a short summary with the questions to ask the client.

  • Cost: similar to claims, and lower if the claims flow already exists, because both use the same connection to your broking system.
  • Good for: brokers who spend the first hour of each renewal looking for information.
  • Watch for: old or missing data. The summary is only as good as your records. The broker still gives the advice.

3. Certificates of currency and endorsements

Clients ask for a certificate or a change, and get an answer from a portal without waiting for a person.

  • Cost: in the same range. It needs a reliable connection to your broking system. See how to connect your broking system.
  • Good for: brokerages with many small, repeat requests.
  • Watch for: the certificate must come from your broking system’s data, not from text that AI writes. AI helps with the request. The system produces the document.

Five rules to keep client data safe

  1. No public chatbots. The OAIC recommends that organisations do not put personal information into publicly available AI tools. Use a business AI service that you control.
  2. Your own cloud account, in Australia. For example, Claude on Amazon Bedrock with the Australian profile keeps requests in Sydney and Melbourne. Bedrock does not store your prompts or use them to train models. Some new models need data sharing with the model provider, so check that setting and turn it off.
  3. A person approves anything that leaves the brokerage. AI drafts and sorts. A person sends to clients and insurers.
  4. Log every AI action. Record what the AI read, what it suggested and who approved it. Insurers, auditors and clients now ask for this.
  5. Update your privacy policy. From 10 December 2026, if a computer program makes or informs decisions that significantly affect people, your privacy policy must say so. Check with your adviser whether your AI flows are covered.

What moves the price

  1. The number of inboxes and email types. One shared claims inbox is simpler than ten brokers’ personal inboxes.
  2. Access to your broking system. AI needs client and policy data to match emails correctly.
  3. The state of your records. Duplicate clients and missing policy numbers lower accuracy.
  4. How much a person must check. More checks at the start, fewer once accuracy is measured.
  5. Security and audit needs. Logs, access control and data location add work, and they are what makes the system safe to use.

How to get to a fixed answer

  1. Discovery session. We look at your inboxes, your broking system and your records, and choose the first flow. You get a short plan and a fixed estimate.
  2. Prototype on test data. Within two weeks, one flow works on test or anonymised data, so you see the result before real client data goes in. If you go ahead, the cost is credited against the build.
  3. First version. We measure accuracy on your own records before go-live, and we show it in a demo before each invoice.

Questions brokers ask

Will AI decide our claims?

No. AI sorts emails, takes out the facts and drafts replies. When you act for the client, you advocate and the insurer decides. When you handle claims under an insurer’s authority, a person in your team still makes each decision.

Can our team use ChatGPT for client emails?

Not with client details in a public tool. The OAIC recommends against it. A business AI service in your own environment gives you the same help with control over the data.

Where does our client data go?

It stays in your own cloud account in Australia. With Claude on Amazon Bedrock and the Australian profile, requests are processed in Sydney and Melbourne, and Bedrock does not store prompts or train on them.

Do we need to change our privacy policy?

Possibly. From 10 December 2026, a privacy policy must describe computer programs that make or inform decisions that significantly affect people. Sorting emails is usually not that. Deciding priority or outcomes may be. Check with your adviser.

How accurate is it?

We do not promise a number before we test it. We measure accuracy on your own records during the build, and you decide what a person must still check.
