Lean AI governance: a practical framework for Australian financial services

Most mid-sized financial services businesses already use AI. Staff use chat tools. Suppliers add AI to products you already pay for. A team wants to put an AI feature in front of customers.

By Anastas Manojlovski · 20 November 2026

The pressure goes two ways. The business wants to move faster. The risk team cannot approve what it cannot see. Without governance, AI work either stops or happens without control.

AI governance fixes that. It does not need a large team or a 40-page policy. It needs clear decisions, made once and applied every time.

Here is the lean framework that we use. It has seven areas. Start with the essentials in each area, then add detail as your AI use grows.

Which rules apply?

Australia has no single AI law for financial services. The rules that apply to your AI are mostly the rules that already apply to your business:

  • APRA CPS 230 Operational Risk Management, in effect since 1 July 2025, makes the board accountable for operational risk. That includes the risk of AI failures, and the risk from service providers who use AI for you.
  • APRA CPS 234 Information Security requires security controls in line with the risk to your information. AI often needs a lot of data, so it is in scope.
  • The Financial Accountability Regime (FAR) requires clear accountability. Each AI system and its risks should map to a named senior executive.
  • ASIC obligations for licensees apply to AI the same as any other process. For example, you must still provide services efficiently, honestly and fairly.
  • The Privacy Act applies to any personal information that AI reads, stores or produces. From 10 December 2026, privacy policies must explain when a computer program uses personal information to make, or substantially help to make, decisions that significantly affect people.

The Australian Government has also consulted on mandatory guardrails for AI in high-risk settings. A framework that you build now prepares you for whatever comes next.

If you operate in more than one country, for example Australia, New Zealand and the United Kingdom, build one framework. Then map each country's rules to it. Do not run three separate frameworks.

1. Purpose and accountability

  • Say why you use AI. Write down how AI supports your strategy and your values. This guides every later decision.
  • Set clear goals for governance, including how you will meet your APRA, ASIC and Privacy Act obligations.
  • Give each AI system a named owner, and map the risks to the executive who is accountable under FAR.
  • Define roles: who approves new uses, who manages the risks, and who reports to the board.

2. Know your AI: inventory and risk

  • Keep an AI register. List each AI system and tool, its owner, what it does, and the data it uses. Include supplier products with AI features.
  • Assess the risk of each use. Use a simple AI risk assessment, with a plan to monitor, report and fix the risks.
  • Assess the impact on customers. Before a customer-facing launch, ask what harm it could cause and to whom.
  • Check for bias and fairness, with extra care for vulnerable customers.

3. Approved tools and uses

  • Approved models and licence types. List the AI providers, models and plans that staff can use, and the ones that they must not use. Business plans and enterprise licences usually give you control of your data. Free and personal plans usually do not.
  • Approved and rejected uses. Publish a short list of uses that are approved, and uses that are not.
  • A path for new ideas. Give staff a simple way to suggest a new use or a new provider, and a clear process to assess it.
  • Personal and company accounts. Company work goes only in company AI accounts. Never in a personal account.
  • Access control. Decide who can use which AI tools and data, by role.
  • Approved connectors. List which systems AI tools can connect to, for example Jira or Confluence, and which they cannot.

4. Data and security

  • Classify and label your data, so staff know what can go into which AI tool.
  • Control where data goes. Decide where AI can process your data, and manage cross-border disclosure. For example, on Amazon Bedrock you can keep Claude requests between AWS regions in Australia.
  • Update your security controls for AI: access, logging, and protection against new attacks such as prompt injection.
  • Tell customers how you use AI with their data. Write a customer AI data policy, and update your terms and conditions where needed.

5. Build, launch and change

  • One end-to-end process to design, build, test and launch AI systems.
  • A pre-launch checklist that every AI system passes before go-live.
  • Be open about AI-generated content. Tell customers when they deal with AI, or when AI produced the content that they see.
  • Plan changes to the AI system and to the framework itself. Do not change them ad hoc.
  • Plan the exit. Know how to turn off, replace or move each AI system.

6. People

  • Train all staff, and check what they learnt. Monthly at the start, then quarterly.
  • Include AI awareness in onboarding for every new staff member.
  • State the consequences for anyone who breaches the policies or works outside the agreed risk limits.
  • Make it easy to speak up. Give staff and customers a clear way to give feedback, make suggestions and report problems.

7. Assurance

  • Test and report regularly, quarterly or six-monthly, on accuracy, incidents and risks.
  • Review suppliers. AI providers change their terms often. Review the terms, and audit key suppliers, quarterly or six-monthly.

A simple rhythm

How often What
Every new staff member AI awareness in onboarding
Monthly at the start Staff training and checks
Quarterly Staff training, testing reports, supplier terms review
Six-monthly Supplier audits, framework review
Before every launch Risk assessment, customer impact assessment, pre-launch checklist

Start lean, then grow

You do not need all of this on day one. A one-page AI register, named owners, a list of approved tools and clear data rules already put you ahead of most businesses. They also unblock your teams, because the rules are clear.
