How to connect your broking system to Xero and your CRM

Your broking system, CRM and Xero each hold part of the client. What each system should own, and three ways to connect them.

By Anastas Manojlovski · 26 November 2026

Contents

  1. The question behind the question
  2. What stays where
  3. Three ways to connect
  4. What moves the effort
  5. How to get to a fixed answer
  6. Questions brokers ask

Short answer. Keep your broking system as the source of truth for clients, policies and trust money. Send Xero one summary journal each day, and give your CRM a read copy of each client. A first version usually takes four to six weeks. The hard part is not Xero. It is how you get data out of your broking system.

The question behind the question

Brokers describe it in the same words: a “spaghetti mess”. The broking system, the CRM, Xero, Outlook and the phone system each hold part of the client. Month-end journals go into Xero by hand. Collections and cash-flow forecasts are not automatic. Certificates of currency and endorsements wait for a person to copy data from one screen to another.

So the question is not “can these systems talk?” Most can. The question is “what should each system own, and which connection is safe enough for client money?”

What stays where

Decide this first. Most integration problems come from two systems that both think they own the same data.

Data Owner (source of truth) Other systems get
Clients and policies Broking system CRM: a read copy, updated daily or on change
Trust money (premiums held for insurers) Broking system Xero: nothing, or one summary balance if your accountant wants it
Commission, fees and GST Broking system Xero: one summary journal each day
Operating costs, payroll, BAS Xero —
Sales activity and follow-ups CRM Broking system: nothing, or a note on the client
Emails and documents Broking system or your document store CRM: a link, not a copy

Trust money stays in the broking system. Broking systems such as Steadfast INSIGHT and JAVLN Platform include trust accounting and bank reconciliation. Keep the trust ledger and the trust bank reconciliation there. If your accountant wants the trust balance in Xero for the balance sheet, send one summary journal to a trust bank account and a trust liability account, and do not reconcile trust in Xero. Check the design with your accountant or auditor, because client money has its own rules under Part 7.8 of the Corporations Act.

Three ways to connect

1. A ready-made connector or automation tool

For example a marketplace add-on, or a tool such as Zapier or Make.

  • Cost: a monthly subscription, plus a few days of set-up.
  • Good for: simple flows, such as a new client in the CRM creating a task.
  • Watch for: money flows, error handling and audit. If a step fails quietly, nobody sees it until month-end.

2. Scheduled export and import

The broking system exports a report each day. A small job reads the file, checks it and sends the result to Xero or the CRM.

  • Cost: a small build. Low running cost.
  • Good for: broking systems with no API, or an API that you cannot get access to.
  • Watch for: reports that change format after an upgrade. The job must check every file and stop when something looks wrong.

3. Direct API integration

A small integration service, in your own cloud account, reads from the broking system’s API and writes to Xero and the CRM.

  • Cost: see the table in what a broker system costs. A first version is usually in the $20k–45k range.
  • Good for: daily journals, client sync, collections and cash-flow reports, and anything you want to automate later with AI.
  • Watch for: API access. Ask your broking system vendor early what they allow, and what security checks they need.

What moves the effort

  1. Access to the broking system. This differs a lot between systems. JAVLN publishes its API documentation on a public developer portal. Steadfast offers an INSIGHT API, and it is moving partners to an API marketplace with security checks. WinBEAT interfaces with most industry providers. Ask your vendor what applies to you.
  2. Platform changes. INSIGHT is becoming Steadfast Apps. Steadfast says existing API integrations are not affected at this stage. Design the integration so that the connection to the broking system is one small part you can change.
  3. Xero’s limits. Xero allows 60 API calls a minute and 5,000 a day for each organisation. Send summaries, not every transaction.
  4. The state of your data. Duplicate clients and different spellings across systems must be matched first.
  5. Who signs off. Every journal that reaches Xero needs an owner who checks it, at least for the first months.

How to get to a fixed answer

  1. Discovery session. We map your systems, your data flows and who owns what. We check API access with your vendor. You get a short plan and a fixed estimate.
  2. Clickable prototype or one working flow. Within two weeks, for example the daily journal into a test Xero organisation. If you go ahead, the cost is credited against the build.
  3. First version. The agreed flows, with logs, alerts and a check before anything posts to Xero. We show it in a demo before each invoice.

Questions brokers ask

Can we connect Steadfast INSIGHT, WinBEAT or JAVLN to Xero?

Usually yes, through the vendor’s API or a scheduled report. Each vendor controls access differently, so we confirm it in the discovery session before you commit.

Does our trust account move to Xero?

No. Keep the trust ledger and the trust reconciliation in your broking system. Xero receives your commission, fees and GST as a summary journal, and, if your accountant wants it, the trust balance as one summary line.

What happens when INSIGHT becomes Steadfast Apps?

Steadfast says existing API integrations are not affected at this stage. We keep the broking-system connection small and separate, so a change affects one part only.

Do we need to replace our CRM?

Usually not. The CRM gets a read copy of each client from the broking system, so your team sees the same details everywhere.

Is Zapier or Make enough?

For simple, low-risk flows, yes. For money and client data, use a connection that checks each step, logs it, and alerts a person when something fails.
