A Claude readiness review before your prototype goes live

Your Claude prototype works in a demo. In two weeks, we tell you if it is ready for production, and what to fix if it is not. You get a written report and a fix plan. If we then do the work, we credit the fee in full.

Start a conversationEmail anastas@beyondzenith.com.au

What you get

  • A fixed price, in two weeks

    You know the cost before the review starts. The review takes two weeks.

  • Accuracy, measured on your data

    We make a test set from your own records and measure how often the output is correct. You get a number, not an opinion.

  • Security findings, each with a fix

    We examine access control, data retention, prompt injection and where your data goes. Each finding has a rating and a fix.

  • The running cost

    We calculate the cost of each request now, and the cost after changes such as model routing and caching.

  • A compliance map

    We compare the system with the Privacy Act, your own AI policy and the rules for your industry, such as APRA CPS 230 if it applies to you.

  • A fix plan you keep

    The work in phases, the effort for each phase, and a clear test for go-live. The plan is yours, whoever does the work.

How it works

  1. First conversation

    You show us the prototype and tell us what it must do. We tell you if a review is the right next step.

  2. Test set

    We select records from your history. Your experts tell us the correct output for each record.

  3. Review

    We run the prototype on the test set, and we examine the code, the security, the cost and the compliance. The work stays inside your own environment.

  4. Report and fix plan

    A rating for each area, the findings, and a costed plan with a test for go-live. Then the decision is yours.

When a review is a good fit

You have a Claude prototype or pilot, and you must decide if it can go to production.

A review also helps when the output is sometimes wrong and nobody can say how often, when the running cost is higher than you expected, or when your risk team asks questions that you cannot answer yet.
